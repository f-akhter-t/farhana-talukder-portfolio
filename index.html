01 / About

Drawn to the parts of AI that have to be trusted.

I'm in my final year of CSE at IIUC, and most of my research asks one question from different angles: when should a model's output be trusted? My thesis with Riktika Talukder tests whether adding patient-case evidence to medical literature makes retrieval-augmented answers more accurate and better calibrated, across three biomedical datasets.

Around it sit projects on hypergraph networks for multi-drug side effects, lesion-guided diabetic retinopathy grading and confidence-driven ant colony routing. On the systems side I've built a shell-scripted LAN game hub, a C++ code checker and passive attack detection in Kali Linux. Right now I'm a backend AI engineer intern at FlyRank AI.

Flagship · ThesisIIUC · Spring 2026

Confidence Calibration in Dual-Source Retrieval-Augmented Biomedical Question Answering

An undergraduate thesis studying how combining medical literature with patient-case evidence affects both the accuracy and the trustworthiness of AI-generated answers in clinical question-answering. Runs controlled experiments across three biomedical datasets to show when a second evidence source helps, when it does not, and why.

with Riktika Talukder · supervised by Zinia Sultana Mukti

HARP-TS

HARP-TS: Confidence-Driven Two-Timescale Parameter Adaptation for ACO Routing in Hierarchical Vehicular Delivery Networks

A routing optimization approach for hierarchical vehicle-delivery networks that adjusts an ant colony algorithm's parameters on two different timescales, guided by how confident the system is in its own routing decisions.
